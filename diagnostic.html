Method, in one paragraph

Every dimension is scored 0–100 from evidence attached to the opportunity: call notes, email threads, meeting records, and buyer-confirmed facts. A claim with no dated source behind it does not raise a score, regardless of how confident the rep is. Because the inputs are fixed and the rules are fixed, the same evidence always produces the same score — nothing here is a vibe check.

Timing & Close Integrity

44 / 100

Question — Does the recorded close date match a decision process the buyer has actually confirmed — budget cycle, contract review, board timing?

Method — Cross-references the CRM close date against buyer-stated timelines found in call notes and email. A date with no buyer-side anchor is treated as a guess, not a fact.

Stakeholder Coverage

46 / 100

Question — How many of the roles required to buy — champion, economic buyer, technical evaluator, legal/procurement — have a named, engaged person attached?

Method — Counts confirmed roles against the roles that opportunities of this size and stage typically require, not against a fixed checklist.

Decision Evidence

50 / 100

Question — Is there a documented decision process: criteria, timeline, and who signs — in the buyer's words, not the rep's assumption?

Method — Looks for buyer-authored or buyer-confirmed statements about how the decision will be made, weighted down when the only source is the seller's notes.

Commercial Evidence

51 / 100

Question — Has pricing, budget, and commercial terms been discussed with someone who controls budget, with a number on record?

Method — Requires a two-sided commercial exchange — a quote sent is not evidence; a quote discussed and responded to is.

Buyer Access

54 / 100

Question — Can the seller reach decision-makers directly, or does every path run through a single proxy?

Method — Measures direct interactions with confirmed decision-makers versus interactions that are only reported second-hand.

Problem & Outcome Strength

55 / 100

Question — Is the business problem specific and quantified — a baseline, a target, a timeframe — or described only in general terms?

Method — Scores rise when the buyer has stated their own numbers; generic pain language from the seller's discovery deck does not count.

Buyer Commitment & Momentum

65 / 100

Question — Has the buyer taken an action that costs them something — internal socialization, a reference call, a signed NDA — versus just staying responsive?

Method — Distinguishes buyer-initiated effort from seller-initiated follow-up; passive responsiveness scores lower than active investment.

Relationship Strength

73 / 100

Question — Is there a multi-threaded, recently active relationship, or does the deal depend on one contact who may go dark?

Method — Combines the number of engaged stakeholders with contact recency; a strong single relationship still scores lower than a threaded one.

Opportunity Reality

77 / 100

Question — Does the opportunity correspond to a real, budgeted initiative the buyer independently describes the same way?

Method — Checks for consistency between what the seller recorded and what the buyer says exists, flagging contradictions between the two.
